# Metformin - Clinical Guidelines

## Drug Class

Biguanide Antidiabetic Agent

## Indications

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (first-line therapy)
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) - off-label
- Prevention of type 2 diabetes in high-risk patients

## Mechanism of Action

- Decreases hepatic glucose production
- Decreases intestinal glucose absorption
- Improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake

## Dosing

### Initial Dosing

- Immediate-release: 500 mg twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals
- Extended-release: 500-1000 mg once daily with evening meal

### Titration

- Increase by 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every 2 weeks
- Maximum dose: 2550 mg/day (immediate-release) or 2000 mg/day (extended-release)

### Renal Dosing

- eGFR 45-59 mL/min/1.73m²: Monitor closely, may continue
- eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²: Initiation not recommended; if already on therapy, reduce dose by 50%
- eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²: Contraindicated

## Contraindications

- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²)
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis
- History of lactic acidosis
- Severe hepatic impairment
- Acute heart failure
- Conditions predisposing to lactic acidosis (sepsis, dehydration, excessive alcohol intake)

## Common Side Effects

- Gastrointestinal: diarrhea (30%), nausea, vomiting, flatulence, abdominal discomfort
- Vitamin B12 deficiency (long-term use, 10-30%)
- Metallic taste

## Serious Adverse Effects

- **Lactic acidosis** (rare but potentially fatal, 0.03 cases per 1000 patient-years)
- Hypoglycemia (rare when used alone, increased risk with insulin or sulfonylureas)

## Drug Interactions

- **Alcohol**: Increased risk of lactic acidosis
- **Contrast dye (iodinated)**: Increased risk of acute renal failure and lactic acidosis
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors** (topiramate, zonisamide): Increased risk of lactic acidosis
- **Cationic drugs** (cimetidine, amiloride): May increase metformin levels
- **Insulin and sulfonylureas**: Increased hypoglycemia risk

## Monitoring

- Renal function: Check eGFR before initiation and at least annually
- HbA1c: Every 3 months until stable, then at least every 6 months
- Vitamin B12 levels: Periodically, especially with long-term use
- Liver function tests if clinically indicated

## Special Considerations

### Contrast Procedures

- **Discontinue metformin** at time of or before iodinated contrast procedure
- May restart 48 hours after procedure if renal function is stable
- Hold for at least 48 hours in patients with eGFR 30-60 mL/min/1.73m²

### Surgery

- Consider holding metformin on day of surgery if patient will be NPO
- May resume when patient is eating and renal function is stable

## Patient Education

- Take with meals to reduce GI side effects
- Do not crush or chew extended-release formulations
- Report signs of lactic acidosis: muscle pain, weakness, difficulty breathing, unusual tiredness, stomach pain
- Inform all healthcare providers about metformin use, especially before procedures with contrast dye
- Maintain adequate hydration
- Limit alcohol consumption
- Does not typically cause hypoglycemia when used alone

## Special Populations

### Elderly (≥65 years)

- Higher risk of lactic acidosis due to age-related decline in renal function
- Monitor renal function closely
- Use conservative initial dosing

### Pregnancy

- Category B (some guidelines support use; insulin remains first-line)
- May continue in women with PCOS or gestational diabetes per specialist recommendation

### Hepatic Impairment

- Avoid in severe hepatic disease (increased lactic acidosis risk)

## Clinical Pearls

- Metformin is weight-neutral or may cause modest weight loss
- Cardiovascular benefits beyond glycemic control
- Starting with low dose and slow titration reduces GI side effects
- Extended-release formulation may improve GI tolerability
- First-line agent for type 2 diabetes per ADA/EASD guidelines
