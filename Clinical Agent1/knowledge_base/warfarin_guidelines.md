# Warfarin - Clinical Guidelines

## Drug Class

Vitamin K Antagonist, Oral Anticoagulant

## Indications

- Atrial fibrillation (stroke prevention)
- Venous thromboembolism (DVT/PE) treatment and prevention
- Mechanical heart valve replacement
- Post-myocardial infarction (selected cases)
- Antiphospholipid syndrome

## Mechanism of Action

Inhibits vitamin K-dependent clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X) and proteins C and S

## Dosing

- Highly individualized based on INR
- Typical initial dose: 5 mg daily (2.5 mg in elderly or high bleeding risk)
- Maintenance dose range: 1-10 mg daily
- Dose adjustments based on INR monitoring

### Target INR Ranges

- Atrial fibrillation, DVT/PE: 2.0-3.0
- Mechanical heart valves: 2.5-3.5 (some valves 2.0-3.0)

## Contraindications

- Active major bleeding
- Recent or planned surgery (CNS, eye, trauma)
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension
- Known hypersensitivity
- Pregnancy (teratogenic)
- Inability to monitor INR reliably
- High fall risk or non-compliance concerns

## Common Side Effects

- Easy bruising
- Minor bleeding (gums, nose)
- Hair loss
- Nausea
- Rash

## Serious Adverse Effects

- **Major bleeding** (intracranial, GI, retroperitoneal)
- Warfarin-induced skin necrosis (rare, first few days)
- Purple toe syndrome
- Osteoporosis (long-term use)

## Drug Interactions (Extensive)

### Increase Anticoagulation (Higher INR/Bleeding Risk)

- **Antibiotics**: Metronidazole, ciprofloxacin, azithromycin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
- **Antifungals**: Fluconazole, miconazole
- **Cardiovascular**: Amiodarone, propafenone
- **NSAIDs**: Aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen
- **Antiplatelets**: Clopidogrel, ticagrelor
- **Supplements**: Fish oil, vitamin E, garlic, ginkgo biloba
- **SSRIs**: Fluoxetine, sertraline

### Decrease Anticoagulation (Lower INR)

- **Vitamin K-rich foods**: Leafy greens (spinach, kale, broccoli)
- **Anticonvulsants**: Carbamazepine, phenytoin
- **Rifampin**
- **St. John's Wort**

## Monitoring

- **INR**:
  - Initially every 2-3 days until stable
  - Then weekly for 1-2 weeks
  - Then every 2-4 weeks when stable
  - More frequent monitoring after dose changes or with interacting medications
- **CBC**: Baseline and if bleeding suspected
- **Liver function**: Baseline
- **Signs of bleeding**: Ongoing assessment

## Management of Elevated INR

### INR 4.5-10 (No Bleeding)

- Hold 1-2 doses, then resume at lower dose
- If INR > 5, consider oral vitamin K 1-2.5 mg

### INR > 10 (No Bleeding)

- Hold warfarin
- Oral vitamin K 2.5-5 mg

### Major Bleeding

- Stop warfarin
- IV vitamin K 10 mg
- Prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) or fresh frozen plasma (FFP)

## Patient Education

- Take at the same time each day (usually evening)
- **Maintain consistent vitamin K intake** (don't avoid, but be consistent)
- Avoid excessive alcohol
- Report any signs of bleeding immediately
- Use soft toothbrush, electric razor
- Inform all healthcare providers about warfarin use
- Carry medical alert identification
- Do NOT take aspirin or NSAIDs without consulting physician
- Many medications interact - always check before starting new drugs or supplements

## Special Populations

### Elderly

- Higher bleeding risk
- More sensitive to warfarin effects
- Start with lower doses (2.5 mg)
- More frequent monitoring

### Pregnancy

- **Contraindicated** (teratogenic, especially first trimester)
- Use low molecular weight heparin instead

### Renal Impairment

- Use with caution; may have higher bleeding risk
- No dose adjustment based solely on renal function
- Monitor INR closely

### Hepatic Impairment

- Higher bleeding risk due to decreased clotting factor synthesis
- Use with extreme caution
- May require lower doses

## Bridging Therapy

When warfarin must be interrupted for procedures:

- Stop warfarin 5 days before procedure
- Bridge with LMWH or unfractionated heparin in high-risk patients
- Resume warfarin evening of procedure or next day if hemostasis achieved

## Dietary Considerations

Foods high in vitamin K (counseling: consistency is key, not avoidance):

- Leafy green vegetables (kale, spinach, collard greens, Swiss chard)
- Broccoli, Brussels sprouts
- Green tea
- Liver

## Reversal Agents

- **Vitamin K**: IV or oral (onset 6-24 hours)
- **Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC)**: Immediate reversal
- **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)**: Alternative if PCC unavailable

## Clinical Pearls

- Narrow therapeutic index requires careful monitoring
- Genetic testing (CYP2C9, VKORC1) may help predict dose requirements
- Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) are alternatives with fewer interactions, no monitoring
- Warfarin remains preferred for mechanical heart valves
- Consistency in diet and medications is more important than avoidance
