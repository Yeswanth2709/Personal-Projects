# Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid) - Clinical Guidelines

## Drug Class

Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID), Antiplatelet Agent

## Indications

- Acute coronary syndrome
- Secondary prevention of myocardial infarction
- Stroke prevention
- Mild to moderate pain
- Fever reduction
- Inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis)

## Dosing

### Cardiovascular Protection

- Low-dose: 81-100 mg once daily
- Acute MI: 160-325 mg initial dose (chewable), then 81-100 mg daily

### Analgesic/Antipyretic

- Adults: 325-650 mg every 4-6 hours as needed
- Maximum: 4000 mg/day

### Anti-inflammatory

- 2400-3600 mg/day in divided doses

## Contraindications

- Active bleeding or bleeding disorder
- Known hypersensitivity to aspirin or NSAIDs
- Children and teenagers with viral infections (Reye's syndrome risk)
- Third trimester of pregnancy
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 10 mL/min)
- Active peptic ulcer disease

## Common Side Effects

- Gastrointestinal: dyspepsia, nausea, GI bleeding, ulceration
- Bleeding tendency (due to antiplatelet effect)
- Tinnitus (dose-related)
- Rash or urticaria

## Serious Adverse Effects

- Major bleeding events
- Hemorrhagic stroke
- Gastrointestinal perforation
- Anaphylaxis/severe allergic reactions
- Reye's syndrome (in children)

## Drug Interactions

- **Anticoagulants** (warfarin, heparin): Increased bleeding risk
- **Other NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, naproxen): Increased GI toxicity, may reduce aspirin's cardioprotective effect
- **Corticosteroids**: Increased GI bleeding risk
- **Methotrexate**: Increased methotrexate toxicity
- **ACE inhibitors**: Reduced antihypertensive effect
- **Alcohol**: Increased GI bleeding risk

## Monitoring

- Signs of bleeding (bruising, black stools, blood in urine)
- Renal function (especially in elderly or those with pre-existing renal impairment)
- Liver function tests if used long-term at high doses
- Hearing changes (tinnitus may indicate toxicity)

## Patient Education

- Take with food or milk to reduce stomach upset
- Report signs of bleeding immediately
- Avoid alcohol consumption
- Inform healthcare providers before surgery or dental procedures
- Do not crush or chew enteric-coated tablets
- Store at room temperature away from moisture

## Special Populations

### Elderly

- Increased risk of GI bleeding and renal impairment
- Use lowest effective dose
- Consider gastroprotection with PPI

### Pregnancy

- Avoid in third trimester (risk of premature closure of ductus arteriosus)
- Use in first and second trimesters only if benefits outweigh risks

### Renal Impairment

- Use with caution; may worsen renal function
- Avoid in severe impairment (CrCl < 10 mL/min)

### Hepatic Impairment

- Use with caution; increased bleeding risk
- Monitor liver function
