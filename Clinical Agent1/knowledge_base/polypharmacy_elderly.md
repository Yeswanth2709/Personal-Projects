# Polypharmacy in Elderly Patients - Clinical Guidelines

## Definition

Polypharmacy is generally defined as the concurrent use of:

- 5 or more medications (most common definition)
- 10 or more medications (major polypharmacy)

## Prevalence

- 40-50% of adults aged ≥65 years take 5+ medications
- 20% take 10+ medications
- Risk increases with multiple chronic conditions

## Risks Associated with Polypharmacy

### Adverse Drug Events (ADEs)

- Risk increases exponentially with number of medications
- 5-9 medications: 50% increased risk
- 10+ medications: 100% increased risk
- ADEs account for 10-20% of geriatric hospital admissions

### Drug-Drug Interactions

- Significantly increased with each additional medication
- May be pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic
- Often predictable and preventable

### Non-Adherence

- More medications = lower adherence
- Complex regimens difficult to manage
- Increased cost burden

### Functional Decline

- Increased fall risk (especially with ≥4 medications)
- Cognitive impairment
- Reduced quality of life

### Prescribing Cascade

- Treating side effects with additional medications
- Example: Treating metoclopramide-induced parkinsonism with levodopa

## High-Risk Medications in Elderly

### Potentially Inappropriate Medications (PIMs) - Beers Criteria

**Anticholinergics**

- Increased delirium, falls, cognitive decline
- Examples: Diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, promethazine

**Benzodiazepines**

- Falls, fractures, cognitive impairment
- Examples: Diazepam, alprazolam (especially long-acting)

**First-Generation Antipsychotics**

- High anticholinergic burden, extrapyramidal effects
- Examples: Haloperidol, chlorpromazine

**NSAIDs**

- GI bleeding, renal impairment, cardiovascular risk
- Use with caution; avoid chronic use

**Muscle Relaxants**

- Sedation, falls
- Examples: Cyclobenzaprine, carisoprodol

**Sliding Scale Insulin**

- Higher hypoglycemia risk without efficacy benefit

**Sulfonylureas (long-acting)**

- Prolonged hypoglycemia
- Examples: Glyburide, chlorpropamide

## Medication Review Process

### Comprehensive Medication Review

1. **List all medications**: Prescription, OTC, supplements, herbal
2. **Assess indication**: Is there a current indication for each medication?
3. **Review evidence**: Is medication evidence-based for the condition?
4. **Evaluate effectiveness**: Is the medication achieving therapeutic goal?
5. **Assess safety**: Consider drug-drug interactions, renal/hepatic function
6. **Consider alternatives**: Are safer alternatives available?
7. **Deprescribe**: Can any medications be discontinued?

### STOPP/START Criteria

- **STOPP** (Screening Tool of Older Persons' Prescriptions): Identifies PIMs
- **START** (Screening Tool to Alert to Right Treatment): Identifies omitted beneficial medications

## Deprescribing Strategies

### General Principles

- Prioritize medications with highest harm potential
- One medication at a time (usually)
- Slow tapering for medications requiring gradual withdrawal
- Monitor for withdrawal effects or disease recurrence
- Involve patient and family in decision-making

### Good Candidates for Deprescribing

- Medications without clear indication
- Medications not achieving therapeutic goals
- Medications with unacceptable side effects
- Duplicate therapy
- Medications treating side effects of other medications
- Preventive medications in patients with limited life expectancy

### Medications Requiring Gradual Tapering

- Benzodiazepines
- Opioids
- Antidepressants
- Antipsychotics
- Beta-blockers
- Corticosteroids
- Proton pump inhibitors (high-dose, long-term)

## Special Considerations in Elderly

### Age-Related Pharmacokinetic Changes

- **Absorption**: Reduced gastric acid, delayed gastric emptying
- **Distribution**: Increased body fat, decreased lean mass and water
- **Metabolism**: Reduced hepatic blood flow and enzyme activity
- **Excretion**: Decreased renal function (even with normal creatinine due to reduced muscle mass)

### Age-Related Pharmacodynamic Changes

- Increased sensitivity to medications
- Altered receptor density and function
- Examples: Increased sensitivity to warfarin, benzodiazepines, opioids

### Renal Function Assessment

- **Serum creatinine unreliable** in elderly due to reduced muscle mass
- **Use eGFR** (Cockcroft-Gault or CKD-EPI) for dosing decisions
- Many medications require renal dose adjustment

## High-Risk Drug Classes Requiring Monitoring

### Anticoagulants

- Warfarin: Increased bleeding risk, monitor INR
- DOACs: Renal dose adjustments critical

### Diabetes Medications

- Insulin, sulfonylureas: Higher hypoglycemia risk
- Metformin: Contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min

### Cardiovascular Medications

- Diuretics: Dehydration, electrolyte imbalance
- Antihypertensives: Orthostatic hypotension, falls

### Psychotropics

- Antidepressants: SIADH, falls
- Antipsychotics: Stroke, mortality risk in dementia

## Fall Risk Medications

Medications increasing fall risk:

- Psychotropics (antidepressants, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines)
- Antihypertensives
- Opioids
- Anticonvulsants
- Hypoglycemic agents

**≥4 medications = significantly increased fall risk**

## Patient and Caregiver Education

- Medication list: Keep updated, bring to all appointments
- Purpose of each medication
- How and when to take medications
- Expected benefits and potential side effects
- When to report concerns
- Use of pill organizers, medication calendars
- Medication disposal of discontinued drugs

## Clinical Pearls

- "Start low, go slow" - initiate at lower doses, titrate gradually
- Regular medication reconciliation at every visit
- Involve pharmacists in medication review
- Consider "medication holidays" for reassessment
- Quality over quantity - fewer medications often better
- Life expectancy matters for preventive medications
- Patient goals and preferences should guide decisions

## Red Flags

- Patient taking medications they can't name or explain
- Multiple prescribers without coordination
- Medications from multiple pharmacies
- Using medications from friends/family
- Crushing or splitting medications not meant to be split
