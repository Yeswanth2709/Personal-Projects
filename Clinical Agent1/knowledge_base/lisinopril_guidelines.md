# Lisinopril - Clinical Guidelines

## Drug Class

Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitor

## Indications

- Hypertension
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)
- Acute myocardial infarction (within 24 hours)
- Diabetic nephropathy

## Mechanism of Action

Inhibits ACE, preventing conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, resulting in:

- Vasodilation (reduced blood pressure)
- Reduced aldosterone secretion (decreased sodium and water retention)
- Cardioprotective and renoprotective effects

## Dosing

### Hypertension

- Initial: 10 mg once daily
- Maintenance: 20-40 mg once daily
- Maximum: 80 mg once daily

### Heart Failure

- Initial: 2.5-5 mg once daily
- Target: 20-40 mg once daily
- Titrate slowly over weeks

### Post-MI

- Initial: 5 mg within 24 hours, then 5 mg after 24 hours, then 10 mg daily
- Maintenance: 10 mg once daily

### Renal Dosing

- CrCl > 30 mL/min: No adjustment
- CrCl 10-30 mL/min: Initial 5 mg daily, max 40 mg daily
- CrCl < 10 mL/min: Initial 2.5 mg daily, max 40 mg daily

## Contraindications

- History of angioedema related to ACE inhibitors
- Hereditary or idiopathic angioedema
- Pregnancy (teratogenic)
- Bilateral renal artery stenosis
- Concomitant use with aliskiren in diabetic patients

## Common Side Effects

- Dry cough (10-15%, most common reason for discontinuation)
- Dizziness, lightheadedness
- Headache
- Fatigue
- Hyperkalemia

## Serious Adverse Effects

- **Angioedema** (0.1-0.7%, potentially life-threatening)
- Acute renal failure (especially in renal artery stenosis)
- Severe hypotension (first-dose effect)
- Hyperkalemia (especially with concurrent potassium supplements or potassium-sparing diuretics)
- Neutropenia/agranulocytosis (rare)

## Drug Interactions

- **Potassium supplements, potassium-sparing diuretics** (spironolactone, amiloride): Hyperkalemia risk
- **NSAIDs**: Reduced antihypertensive effect, increased renal impairment risk
- **Lithium**: Increased lithium levels and toxicity
- **Diuretics**: Enhanced hypotensive effect (may be therapeutic)
- **Aliskiren**: Contraindicated in diabetes, increased hyperkalemia and hypotension risk
- **Sacubitril**: Contraindicated (angioedema risk)

## Monitoring

- **Blood pressure**: Before and after dose changes
- **Renal function** (creatinine, eGFR): Baseline, 1-2 weeks after initiation, after dose changes
- **Potassium**: Baseline, 1-2 weeks after initiation, periodically
- **Signs of angioedema**: Ongoing vigilance

## Patient Education

- May take with or without food
- Take at same time each day
- Do not stop abruptly
- Report persistent dry cough (may require switch to ARB)
- Report swelling of face, lips, tongue (angioedema - seek emergency care)
- Change positions slowly to avoid dizziness
- Avoid potassium supplements unless directed by physician
- Adequate hydration, especially during exercise or hot weather
- **Pregnancy**: Use effective contraception; stop immediately if pregnant

## Special Populations

### Elderly

- More sensitive to hypotensive effects
- Higher risk of renal impairment
- Start with lower doses
- Monitor closely

### Pregnancy

- **Contraindicated** (Category D)
- Fetal toxicity: oligohydramnios, renal dysfunction, skull hypoplasia
- Discontinue as soon as pregnancy detected
- Alternative: methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine

### Renal Impairment

- Dose adjustment required
- Monitor renal function closely
- May see transient increase in creatinine (up to 30% acceptable)
- Discontinue if creatinine increases > 30% or hyperkalemia develops

### Heart Failure

- Initiate at low doses and titrate slowly
- Target evidence-based doses for mortality benefit
- May cause transient hypotension or worsening renal function during titration

## First-Dose Effect

- Risk of severe hypotension, especially:
  - Volume-depleted patients
  - Patients on high-dose diuretics
  - Severe heart failure
- Consider holding diuretic or reducing dose before initiating ACE inhibitor

## ACE Inhibitor Cough

- Dry, persistent, non-productive cough
- Occurs in 10-15% of patients
- More common in women
- Usually develops within weeks to months
- Resolves within 1-4 weeks after discontinuation
- **Management**: Switch to ARB (angiotensin receptor blocker)

## Clinical Pearls

- Cardioprotective and renoprotective independent of blood pressure lowering
- First-line agent for hypertension with diabetes, heart failure, or post-MI
- Allow 2-4 weeks to see full antihypertensive effect
- Transient increase in creatinine (< 30%) is acceptable and does not require discontinuation
- ARBs (like losartan) are alternatives if cough or angioedema occur

## Black Box Warning

- **Pregnancy Category D**: Can cause injury and death to developing fetus
- Discontinue as soon as pregnancy is detected
